What We Believe

The Second London Baptist Confession of Faith

First published in 1677 and publicly owned by the Particular Baptist churches of England in 1689, this confession is the doctrinal standard of our church.

Why a Confession?

Scripture alone is our rule. A confession helps us say what it teaches.

A confession of faith is not a rival to the Bible. It is a church's public, carefully-worded summary of what it believes the Bible teaches. It guards our pulpit, unites our members, and tells our neighbors plainly where we stand.

The 1689 stands in the line of the Westminster Confession (1646) and the Savoy Declaration (1658), sharing their Reformed doctrine of God, salvation and Scripture, while confessing a Baptist understanding of the church, the covenant of grace and believer's baptism.

Our elders subscribe to the Confession and teach in accordance with it, so that the whole church may grow together in the knowledge of God.

We commend it to you — read it alongside your Bible, with the proof texts open, and you will find a rich, warm, God-centered summary of the faith.

Chapter I, Paragraph 1

“The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience.”

The Thirty-Two Chapters

The Confession at a glance

I

Of the Holy Scriptures

Scripture is the only sufficient, certain and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith and obedience — inspired by God and complete.

2 Tim. 3:15–17; 2 Pet. 1:19–21

II

Of God and of the Holy Trinity

There is one only living and true God, infinite in being and perfection, subsisting in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

Deut. 6:4; Matt. 28:19; 2 Cor. 13:14

III

Of God's Decree

God from all eternity freely and unchangeably ordained whatever comes to pass, yet without being the author of sin.

Isa. 46:10; Eph. 1:3–11; Rom. 9

IV

Of Creation

God the Father, Son and Spirit created the world out of nothing in the space of six days, and all very good; man was made male and female in His image.

Gen. 1–2; John 1:2–3; Heb. 11:3

V

Of Divine Providence

God upholds, directs, disposes and governs all creatures and all things, from the greatest to the least, by His most wise and holy providence.

Heb. 1:3; Matt. 10:29–31; Rom. 8:28

VI

Of the Fall of Man, of Sin, and of the Punishment Thereof

Our first parents fell from original righteousness; in Adam all became dead in sin and are by nature children of wrath.

Gen. 3; Rom. 5:12–19; Eph. 2:1–3

VII

Of God's Covenant

Man could never enjoy God except by His voluntary condescension, which He has expressed by way of covenant — the covenant of grace revealed in the gospel.

Heb. 8; Gal. 3; Gen. 3:15

VIII

Of Christ the Mediator

God chose the Lord Jesus, His only begotten Son, to be Mediator between God and man — Prophet, Priest and King; truly God and truly man in one person.

1 Tim. 2:5; Heb. 1–2; John 1:14

IX

Of Free Will

Man by his fall has wholly lost all ability of will to any spiritual good accompanying salvation; only grace sets him free to will what is good.

John 6:44; Rom. 8:7; Phil. 2:13

X

Of Effectual Calling

Those whom God has predestinated to life, He is pleased in His appointed time effectually to call by His Word and Spirit out of death into life.

Rom. 8:30; Eph. 2:1–8; John 6:37

XI

Of Justification

God freely justifies those He calls — not by infusing righteousness, but by pardoning their sins and accounting them righteous for Christ's sake alone, received by faith alone.

Rom. 3:24–28; 4:5–8; Phil. 3:8–9

XII

Of Adoption

All the justified are made partakers of the grace of adoption: they receive the Spirit of adoption and cry, “Abba, Father.”

Gal. 4:4–6; Rom. 8:15–17; Eph. 1:5

XIII

Of Sanctification

Those united to Christ are further sanctified, really and personally, through the virtue of His death and resurrection, by His Word and Spirit dwelling in them.

1 Thess. 5:23; 2 Cor. 3:18; Rom. 6

XIV

Of Saving Faith

The grace of faith is the work of the Spirit of Christ in the heart; its principal acts are accepting, receiving and resting upon Christ alone.

Eph. 2:8; Heb. 11; John 1:12

XV

Of Repentance unto Life and Salvation

Saving repentance is an evangelical grace whereby a person, sensible of the evil of sin, turns from it to God with godly sorrow and purpose to walk with Him.

Acts 11:18; 2 Cor. 7:10–11; Ezek. 36:31

XVI

Of Good Works

Good works are only such as God has commanded in His Word; they are the fruits and evidences of a true and lively faith.

Eph. 2:10; James 2:18–22; Titus 2:11–14

XVII

Of the Perseverance of the Saints

Those whom God has accepted in the Beloved can neither totally nor finally fall from grace, but shall persevere to the end and be eternally saved.

John 10:28–29; Phil. 1:6; 1 Pet. 1:5

XVIII

Of the Assurance of Grace and Salvation

True believers may in this life be certainly assured that they are in a state of grace, and may rejoice in the hope of the glory of God.

1 John 5:13; Rom. 8:16; 2 Pet. 1:10

XIX

Of the Law of God

The moral law, summarized in the Ten Commandments, forever binds all people, justified and unjustified — not as a covenant of works, but as a rule of life.

Exod. 20; Matt. 5:17–19; Rom. 3:31

XX

Of the Gospel, and of the Extent of the Grace Thereof

Since the covenant of works was broken, God has been pleased to give the promise of Christ, the seed of the woman, as the means of calling the elect.

Gen. 3:15; Rom. 10:14–17; Rev. 13:8

XXI

Of Christian Liberty and Liberty of Conscience

Christ has purchased for believers freedom from the guilt of sin and the curse of the law; God alone is Lord of the conscience.

Gal. 5:1; Rom. 14:4; James 4:12

XXII

Of Religious Worship and the Sabbath Day

God may be worshiped only in the way He has appointed in Scripture. The Lord's Day is the Christian Sabbath, to be kept holy to the Lord.

Deut. 12:32; John 4:24; Rev. 1:10

XXIII

Of Lawful Oaths and Vows

A lawful oath is a part of religious worship wherein the person swearing calls God to witness what he asserts or promises.

Deut. 6:13; Heb. 6:16; Ps. 15:4

XXIV

Of the Civil Magistrate

God has ordained civil magistrates to be under Him, over the people, for His own glory and the public good; we owe them honor and lawful obedience.

Rom. 13:1–7; 1 Pet. 2:13–17; 1 Tim. 2:1–2

XXV

Of Marriage

Marriage is to be between one man and one woman, for the mutual help of husband and wife, the increase of mankind, and the preventing of uncleanness.

Gen. 2:24; Matt. 19:4–6; Heb. 13:4

XXVI

Of the Church

The catholic or universal church consists of the whole number of the elect; Christ is its only Head. Local churches gather under elders and deacons.

Eph. 1:22–23; Col. 1:18; Acts 20:17, 28

XXVII

Of the Communion of Saints

All saints united to Christ by His Spirit and by faith have fellowship with Him and are united to one another in love, sharing gifts and graces.

1 John 1:3; Eph. 4:15–16; Heb. 10:24–25

XXVIII

Of Baptism and the Lord's Supper

Baptism and the Lord's Supper are ordinances of positive and sovereign institution, appointed by Christ, to be continued in His church to the end of the world.

Matt. 28:19–20; 1 Cor. 11:26

XXIX

Of Baptism

Baptism is a sign of fellowship with Christ in His death and resurrection, rightly administered to those who actually profess repentance and faith — by immersion.

Rom. 6:3–5; Acts 2:41; 8:36–38

XXX

Of the Lord's Supper

The Supper was instituted by Christ for the perpetual remembrance of His sacrifice; worthy receivers feed spiritually upon Christ crucified by faith.

1 Cor. 10:16–17; 11:23–26

XXXI

Of the State of Man after Death, and of the Resurrection of the Dead

At death the souls of the righteous are received into paradise with Christ; at the last day the dead shall be raised with the selfsame bodies.

2 Cor. 5:1–8; 1 Cor. 15; John 5:28–29

XXXII

Of the Last Judgment

God has appointed a day wherein He will judge the world in righteousness by Jesus Christ; the righteous enter everlasting life, the wicked everlasting punishment.

Acts 17:31; Matt. 25:31–46; 2 Thess. 1:7–10

The Baptist Catechism

Alongside the Confession we use the Baptist Catechism (1693) — a question-and-answer summary of the faith for teaching children and new believers in the home and in our Keiki classes.

Question 1

Who is the first and chiefest being?

God is the first and chiefest being.

In Brief

Our statement of faith

The Bible

The entire Bible, Genesis to Revelation, is the inspired, inerrant and authoritative Word of God.

The Trinity

One living God eternally exists in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

Jesus Christ

Conceived by the Spirit, born of the virgin Mary, He lived perfectly, died as our substitute, rose bodily, ascended, and will come again to judge the living and the dead.

Salvation

All are sinners. Salvation is by the regenerating work of the Spirit, through repentance and faith alone in Christ, by the shed blood of the Lamb.

The Church

Christ builds His church by the preaching of the Word, fellowship, the ordinances and prayer, and sends it to make disciples of all nations.
